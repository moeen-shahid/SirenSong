    What is real here
    
    
    Real
      
      
    • The coastline, from OpenStreetMap land polygons.
      • 
      
    • The live hydrophone panel, when the page is served by the SirenSong server: real underwater sound from the Salish Sea, classified as it arrives.
      • 
      
    • The ships, when the page is served by the SirenSong server: live positions, names, speeds and courses from Finland's open AIS feed, refreshed every 15 seconds.
      • 
      
    • The test recordings in the listening room, where bundled: real whales recorded by hydrophone. The detector never trains on them unless you label one.
      • 
      
    • The detector: audio synthesised at 8 kHz, a 512-point FFT, and a neural network (432 features, 32 hidden units) trained from scratch when the page loads and retrained by your labels.
      • 
      
    • Lethality by speed, from the logistic curve in Vanderlaan and Taggart (2007).
      • 
    
    
    
    Derived, not fixed
      
      
    • Hydrophone sites are chosen beside the busiest water on the chart you pick.
      • 
      
    • Whale home ranges are chosen from the same traffic picture.
      • 
      
    • When there is no live feed, simulated lanes are the shortest water routes between the chart's open edges and its ports.
      • 
      
    • Talk scenes are written live by Claude where the page can reach it, and come from a written deck of 48 elsewhere, dealt so none repeats until all have been used.
      • 
    
    
    
    Simulated
      
      
    • The whales. No public live whale feed exists, and none of these three species lives in the Baltic. Its only resident cetacean is the harbour porpoise, so the cast are visitors.
      • 
      
    • Whale calls are simplified stand-ins, and sound spreads by plain spherical spreading.
      • 
      
    • A real ship never hears your advisory. With "what if ships obeyed" on, the chart shows a twin of each advised ship slowing down, and a ghost outline where the real ship is.
      • 
      
    • The volunteers are a random number generator.
      •
